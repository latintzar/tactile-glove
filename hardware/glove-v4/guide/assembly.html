Bela glove · V4 handoff

Connect the cuff, soft sensors, camera, and hand tile.

A factory populated cuff meets six keyed tactile branches, a camera FPC, two timestamped IMUs, and a removable glove harness. This guide shows the physical order and the checks that make each connection inspectable.

The tactile core keeps the V3 anatomical cell map: I1–I3, M1–M3, R1–R3, L1–L3, T1–T2, P1–P4, H1–H2. V4 adds the camera, a cuff IMU at 0x6A, a dorsal-hand IMU at 0x6B, storage, and the top control surface.

One physical system, one data path

The hand tile is a separate keyed branch on the 400 kHz I²C harness. The camera FPC and tactile branches enter the cuff; the controller records raw values, timestamps, transport gaps, and power quality with the data.

Prepare the right things at the right bench

Supplier bench

Populate, label, inspect

  1. Populate the cuff board and label J5–J10, J14/J_CAM, J15 hand-tile connector, and J16 on the assembly drawing.
  2. Build six keyed tactile branches and record pin-to-pin continuity plus adjacent isolation.
  3. Keep the camera FPC tail unlatched until its contact side and pin 1 are matched to the supplied module drawing.
  4. Fit the button cap, RGB diffuser, camera restraint, and battery restraint without stressing the board or FPC.
Glove bench

Laminate, route, plug

  1. Print the V3 cut template at 100% and fit paper coupons to the actual glove.
  2. Build each cell with the marked PVDF signal face outward toward the contact side.
  3. Route each four-wire bundle along the side/back, bridge it 15–20 mm behind the cell, and leave a 12–15 mm flex loop.
  4. Plug only the named keyed branch, then seat the hand tile over the third metacarpal.

Seat the V4 cuff interfaces

Dual IMU

One rigid reference, one moving tile

Cuff: U61 is soldered on the main PCB at the camera seat, address 0x6A. Hand: U1/U_IMU_HAND sits on the thin rigid tile over the third metacarpal, address 0x6B; its tile connector J1 mates to the main-board J15 remote connector. Put the tile arrow toward the middle finger and its plug toward the wrist.

J15-to-J1 pin order is 1 GND, 2 3V3_SYS, 3 I2C_SDA, 4 I2C_SCL. Route the 100 mm keyed cable above the wrist in a shallow S loop and fasten the jacket 8 mm behind both plugs.

Camera and storage

Verify orientation before power

Camera: J14/J_CAM is a 24-contact bottom-contact 0.5 mm FFC socket. Match the supplied tail contact side and pin 1, then orient the lens toward the near-finger view.

Storage: J16/J_SD is the push-push microSD socket with independent card detect. Insert the card to the socket marking; the controlled SD rail and card-current assumption remain first-article checks.

Top control

One button, one RGB state surface

SW2 is active low on GPIO2. Hold 800 ms and release to wake; continue to 3 s for pairing; hold 1.5 s while running and release to sleep. D22 is a common-anode RGB with active-low channels: blue startup/pairing, green ready/collecting, amber low or unqualified battery, red double flash ADC trouble.

Power checkpoint

Record the battery configuration

The current contract selects the protected Jauch 250480 6.7 Ah pack (6.5 Ah minimum), with a separate battery temperature sensor. The factory prepares the AWG22 battery harness using the direct or specified extension variant, then mates the keyed Molex power connector: pin 1 positive/red and pin 2 negative/black. The board includes the specified 2.5 A input fuse. Polarity, route length, voltage drop and thermal acceptance are recorded before delivery. Continuity and isolation checks use an unpowered dummy harness; the battery leads stay live when attached to the pack. The selected pack fits the current CAD battery envelope. The conservative energy scenario is 6.019 hours; physical six-hour endurance, temperature, storage capacity, and rail acceptance remain first-article tests. The current enclosure envelope is 112 × 104 × 45.5 mm and the estimated cuff mass is 218–233 g before glove and external wiring. Record the actual pack, dimensions, lead arrangement, and charging temperature limits with the first article.
